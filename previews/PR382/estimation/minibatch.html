Mini-batching ​

For data sets with many individuals, each iteration of an estimator can use a random subset of the individuals instead of all of them. In NoLimits this is a wrapper around the estimation method:

julia
using NoLimits
using Random

res = fit_model(dm, MiniBatch(Laplace(); schedule = 8); rng = Xoshiro(1))

MiniBatch works with MLE, MAP, Laplace, FOCEI, GHQuadrature, VEM, SAEM and MCEM. The result is the FitResult of the wrapped method, so every accessor, plot and uncertainty method applies unchanged.

Batches and Schedules ​

The unit of selection is a random-effect batch: the individuals that share a random-effect level (for a single random effect on the id column, one batch per individual). For MLE and MAP every individual is one batch. The schedule keyword selects the batches of each iteration:

  • an Int m: a random subset of min(m, nbatches) batches, drawn without replacement in every iteration;

  • a callable (nbatches::Int, iter::Int, rng) -> indices returning any iterable of Int (duplicates are ignored), for example a deterministic cycle:

    julia
    cycle(nbatches, iter, rng) = [mod1(iter, nbatches)]
method = MiniBatch(Laplace(); schedule = cycle)

    A stateful callable is shared by every fit that uses the same wrapper object (including all Multistart starts), so pass a fresh instance per fit;

  • :all: every batch in every iteration. The wrapped method then gives exactly the unwrapped fit.

All subsets are drawn from the rng passed to fit_model (Multistart gives each start its own forked rng), so seeded fits are reproducible, and threaded and serial runs agree.

Gradient-Based Methods and VEM ​

For MLE, MAP, Laplace, FOCEI, GHQuadrature and VEM one subset B is drawn per optimizer iteration, and the objective and gradient of that iteration both use it. The contribution of B is multiplied by nbatches / |B|, so the objective and its gradient are unbiased estimates of the full-data objective and gradient. Priors, penalty and extra_objective are global terms and are added once, unscaled. After the fit, the reported objective is evaluated on all batches at the fitted parameters.

The objective is stochastic, so line-search optimizers are inappropriate. Unless the wrapped method was given its own optimizer, MiniBatch replaces the default LBFGS by Optimisers.Adam(0.01) with optim_kwargs = (; maxiters = 1000); save_best = false is added for Optimisers.jl rules (the best single-minibatch objective is not a meaningful criterion). The optimizer and its keywords can also be set on the wrapper:

julia
using Optimisers
method = MiniBatch(
    FOCEI(); schedule = 16, optimizer = Optimisers.Adam(0.005),
    optim_kwargs = (; maxiters = 3000)
)

A non-Optimisers.jl optimizer warns, and it must support Optimization.jl callbacks (the callback advances the schedule). With Optimisers.jl rules, finite bounds (model bounds or lb/ub of the wrapped method) are enforced by projecting each update onto the box. AcceptedWarmStarts requires schedule = :all.

For VEM (stochastic variational EM) the objective is -(K/|B|) Σ_{i∈B} w_i ELBO_i(θ, κ_i) over the K batches with random effects (w_i the likelihood weights, 1 without a weight column), plus the unscaled penalty and extra_objective. Its gradient is the unbiased estimate of the gradient of the full negative ELBO with respect to the fixed effects and all variational parameters: the variational parameters κ_i of an unselected batch do not enter it and get a zero gradient. The full objective is separable in the κ_i, so this stochastic gradient targets the same optimum as the full-batch fit. With refine = :auto the importance-sampling refinement (a full-data pass per iteration) is skipped under mini-batching; refine = true still runs it. With an Optimisers.jl rule the curvature preconditioning of the fixed effects is off, so the rule's steps act on the transformed fixed-effect scale.

SAEM and MCEM ​

For SAEM and MCEM the schedule selects the batches whose random effects are sampled in the E-step of each iteration. The em_variant keyword chooses the M-step:

  • :incremental (default; incremental EM, Neal and Hinton 1998): the batches that were not selected keep their draws from the iteration that last visited them, and the M-step uses all batches. This trades a small bias from draws made under an older θ for a cheaper E-step.

  • :scaled: the M-step uses only the selected batches, with their likelihood weights multiplied by nbatches / |B|, an unbiased estimate of the full-data Q-function. The M-step cost then also scales with |B|. For SAEM it requires mstep_sa_on_params = true (the default) and no custom suffstats. The reported objective is the scaled Q of the last minibatch.

julia
res = fit_model(dm, MiniBatch(SAEM(); schedule = 30); rng = Xoshiro(1))
res = fit_model(dm, MiniBatch(MCEM(); schedule = 30, em_variant = :scaled); rng = Xoshiro(1))

Choice of the Default ​

The default em_variant = :incremental was chosen from a screening comparison (5 seeds per configuration, default SAEM() and MCEM(maxiters = 60), a quarter of the batches per iteration, 4 threads) on four simulated models: a linear Gaussian model with a correlated random intercept and slope (120 individuals x 8 observations), an exponential decay with log-normal amplitude and rate (100 x 6), a Poisson model with a random intercept (150 x 5), and a Michaelis-Menten ODE with a random maximal rate (60 x 6). The error is the mean over parameters of |θ - θ_full| / max(|θ_full|, 0.1) on the transformed scale, where θ_full is the mean of the five full-data fits of the same method; the seed standard deviation is averaged over parameters.

The incremental variant has the smaller error in 6 of 8 model/method pairs (MCEM on the Michaelis-Menten model is a tie, 0.154 against 0.150) and the smaller seed standard deviation in 7 of 8. The exception is SAEM on the Michaelis-Menten model, where the incremental fit had not converged in the weakly identified Km after 300 iterations; with maxiters = 1200 it reached the full-data value. The scaled variant has 1.4 to 3.9 times lower wall time, because its M-step also uses only the selected batches, but its final iterate is noisier; MCEM, which has no stochastic-approximation averaging, ends with errors of 0.06 to 0.15 and its convergence test fires early on the noise. With the iteration budget multiplied by nbatches / |B| (the same number of E-step draws per individual as the full fit), the incremental variant had the smaller error in all 8 pairs. The incremental variant evaluates the M-step on all batches, so its time per iteration was 77 to 90 percent of the full fit for these models; it saves more when the E-step dominates the cost of an iteration.

API ​

NoLimits.MiniBatch Type
julia
MiniBatch(method; schedule, optimizer = nothing, optim_kwargs = nothing,
          em_variant = :incremental) <: FittingMethod

Mini-batch wrapper around an estimation method: each iteration uses a random subset of the random-effect batches (individuals linked by random effects; for MLE/MAP every individual is one batch). Fit with fit_model(dm, MiniBatch(method; schedule = ...)). Supported methods: MLE, MAP, Laplace, FOCEI, GHQuadrature, VEM, SAEM, MCEM.

Keyword Arguments

  • schedule: which batches are used per iteration.

    • Int: a random subset of that size, drawn without replacement each iteration (min(m, nbatches) batches).

    • Any callable (nbatches::Int, iter::Int, rng) -> indices returning an iterable of Int (a Vector{Int}, a range, ...): the batches of iteration iter. Duplicates are ignored. A stateful callable is shared by every fit that uses this wrapper (including all Multistart starts), so pass a fresh instance per fit.

    • :all: every batch every iteration; the wrapped method then behaves exactly like the unwrapped one.

  • optimizer = nothing: outer optimizer of MLE, MAP, Laplace, FOCEI, GHQuadrature and VEM while mini-batching. nothing keeps the method's optimizer, except that the method's default optimizer becomes Optimisers.Adam(0.01) when schedule !== :all. A non-Optimisers.jl optimizer warns (the objective is stochastic); it must support Optimization.jl callbacks.

  • optim_kwargs = nothing: solve keywords for that optimizer. nothing keeps the method's optim_kwargs, or uses (; maxiters = 1000) when the default Adam replaces the default optimizer and the method's optim_kwargs are its defaults. With an Optimisers.jl rule, maxiters = 1000 and save_best = false are added when not given.

  • em_variant = :incremental: mini-batch form of SAEM/MCEM (ignored by the other methods). Both draw new random effects only for the selected batches.

    • :incremental (incremental EM, Neal & Hinton 1998): the other batches keep their draws from their last visit, and the M-step uses all batches.

    • :scaled: the M-step uses only the selected batches, with their likelihood weights multiplied by nbatches / length(selected) (an unbiased estimate of the full-data Q-function). For SAEM it requires mstep_sa_on_params = true and no custom suffstats. The reported objective is then the scaled Q of the last minibatch.

For the gradient-based methods and VEM one subset is drawn per optimizer iteration, the objective and gradient of that iteration share it, and the selected batches are scaled by nbatches / length(selected) so the objective is an unbiased estimate of the full-data objective; priors, penalty and extra_objective are not scaled. The reported objective is re-evaluated on all batches at the fitted parameters. With Optimisers.jl rules finite bounds are enforced by projecting each update onto the box. For VEM the variational parameters of unselected batches get a zero gradient (the unbiased stochastic gradient of the full ELBO); refine = :auto skips the full-data refinement, and with an Optimisers.jl rule the fixed effects are not curvature-preconditioned.

All subsets are drawn from the fit rng (Multistart gives each start its own forked rng), so seeded fits are reproducible and threaded and serial runs agree. The result is the wrapped method's FitResult: get_method returns the wrapped method with its internal schedule set.

julia
fit_model(dm, MiniBatch(Laplace(); schedule = 8); rng = Xoshiro(1))
fit_model(dm, MiniBatch(SAEM(); schedule = 20, em_variant = :scaled))
